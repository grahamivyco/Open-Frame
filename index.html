Park n Go

A mobile app that lets event-goers reserve parking before they leave home, so the night doesn't start with circling the block.

Sole prototyper

Background

Parking is the one part of a night out no one plans for.

Everyday parking already had plenty of apps, so we narrowed in on the gap no one had solved: event and concert parking. Fans plan every part of a show except where they'll park.

How might we take the most stressful part of attending an event and make it easy?

Discover

A stronger signal than we expected.

A survey and four in-depth interviews kept landing on the same problem, parking for events and concerts.

All work Responsive Redesign · Nonprofit

Hmong Cultural Center

A responsive redesign for a Saint Paul cultural center, so the people it serves can find classes and resources without fighting the site.

Role

Sole prototyper · research

Scope

Discovery, strategy & design

Timeline

3-week bootcamp
Overview

Designing for a real community hub.

Over a three-week UX bootcamp, my team partnered with the Hmong Cultural Center in Saint Paul. The center teaches everything from traditional embroidery to citizenship. We wanted to refresh the branding and simplify the site structure so people could get to its resources faster.

The approach

The Double Diamond.

Four phases (Discover, Define, Develop, Deliver) took us from a wide-open problem to a focused redesign for mobile and desktop.

01

Discover

A heuristic audit of the current site plus surveys and interviews. There's no direct competitor, so we studied sites serving similar communities and asked new users what a good first visit would look like.

02

Define

We met Mai Yer Xiong, a first-generation immigrant with limited English. She needed a more visual site with clear icons and a quick way to English-language and citizenship classes.

03

Develop

The old sitemap buried the most important resources. We simplified the IA, mapped a fast user flow to language classes, and moved from paper to mid-fidelity wireframes with a bright, high-contrast UI kit.

04

Deliver

We went from mid to high fidelity in three days. The hero pushes signups, the navigation is simpler and color-coded, and every section has a branded banner on mobile and desktop.

Discover & define

Listening first.

Reflections

The system did the heavy lifting.

Consistent color, type and layout kept the site readable for English-language learners. It also gave us room to bring Hmong cultural elements into the identity and put ESL and citizenship classes up front.

Three weeks for a full redesign meant cutting hard and working fast as a team. We shipped a high-fidelity prototype for mobile and desktop.

Wellness in the Woods

Wellness in the Woods offers free mental-health peer support, remotely, 24/7. Over time its brand had become uneven and fragmented, and it lost the voice that made Wellness different. As Senior Design Lead & Marketing Officer, I led a full overhaul. I replaced the disjointed system with one complete package, a scalable design system that works across every subdivision and reconnects the brand to the mission.

Role

Senior Design Lead & Marketing Officer

Scope

Full brand overhaul, design system & marketing

Timeline

Aug 2023 – Mar 2026

Tools

Canva · Adobe CC · Meta Business Suite · Google Ads

Background

A brand that lost its voice.

Wellness in the Woods runs free peer support for Minnesota residents, remotely and around the clock. It has grown into several subdivisions, including the Virtual Peer Support Network, the Warmline, Recovery in the Woods, Recovery on the Road and a podcast. Over the years each one drifted into its own look and voice. The result was an uneven, fragmented system, and the warmth that sets Wellness apart got lost along the way.

How might we build one design system that keeps every subdivision connected to the mission?

Discover

The voice had drifted.

I lined up a month of posts, flyers and newsletters side by side. VPSN, the Warmline, Recovery in the Woods, Recovery on the Road and the podcast each spoke in their own way, and none of them led with what makes Wellness different.

5+subdivisions, each with its own look and voice
The problem“

The mission was buried at the bottom of the page.

24/7free, remote support, the offer the brand needed to lead with
The problem“

Nothing looked like it came from the same place.

1designer owning design and marketing for the whole organization
The problem“

Staff couldn't post without a designer.

Define

Who we're designing for.

Two groups had to be served at once. People looking for support, and the staff and volunteers who speak for Wellness every week.

Audience 01
P
Peer participantMinnesota resident, 18+ · looking for support

Needs to find a session quickly and feel safe joining. The voice has to sound warm and human.

Goals
  • See this week's sessions at a glance
  • Join by link or phone in one step
  • Feel welcome before showing up
Frustrations
  • Schedules that change format every week
  • Small, hard-to-read text
  • No clear way in
Audience 02
S
Staff & volunteersProgram leads · not designers

Post schedules, flyers and announcements every week, and need them to look right without waiting on design.

Goals
  • Update a template in minutes
  • Stay on-brand without guessing
  • Reuse what already works
Frustrations
  • Starting from a blank page
  • No guidelines to check against
  • Waiting on a designer for every post
Ideate

One voice, many programs.

Patching individual posts wouldn't fix a disjointed system, so I did a full overhaul. I set shared type, color, photography and tone, then built them into a complete package of templates and guidelines. VPSN, the Warmline and the podcast each keep their own content while sounding like the same organization.

  • One voice across every subdivision
  • Faster content production for staff
  • Better accessibility and readability
  • Templates staff reuse every week
Design

Warm and easy to read.

Sage and bark tones from the woods, a bold Staatliches headline and plain Lato body text. It looks like the place it's named after.

Schedules you can read in a second.

I redid the monthly, weekly and daily VPSN calendars in the same voice and design system. Sessions, times and the join link sit in the same place every time, so regulars know where to look and staff just swap in the new week.

How might we make the Guild feel as warm online as it does in the room, and make joining the obvious next step?

System

Built so volunteers can keep it.

Wild Apricot can't publish from code, so every change follows the same four steps from the repo to the live site.

01

One source of truth

Each page is plain markup, and all styling lives in one stylesheet. No page carries its own styles, so nothing drifts out of sync.

02

Design check

A script checks every change: sizes and fonts only through tokens, colours only from the palette, and no class left without a style.

03

Build

A build step strips maintainer notes from paste-ready copies and bundles the header and footer, so volunteers paste one file per gadget.

04

Paste & verify

The stylesheet is pasted into Wild Apricot's CSS tab, because its security policy blocks outside stylesheets. Native forms are checked in the browser console before any selector is written.
